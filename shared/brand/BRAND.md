# TumTum — Brand Identity v2.0

Rebuilt from scratch, August 2026. Visual direction derived from MTV's variable-logo system
(Manhattan Design, 1981) and the channel's 80s/90s on-air graphics.

The full visual brand book, with live logo variations, lives as a published artifact.
This file is the canonical text reference; `tokens.json` and `tokens.css` are the machine-readable
source of truth for colour and type.

---

## 1. The insight

`Tum-tum` is the Brazilian onomatopoeia for a heartbeat. Every person in the target market already
knows how to pronounce the brand and already knows what it means. The job is not to teach the name —
it is to give it a body.

The name also carries structure: **two beats, and they are different.** One loud, one soft. The
identity is therefore always two elements, never one.

### The MTV lesson

In 1981 Manhattan Design delivered a logo whose M had no fixed colour — it was a container. What went
inside changed every thirty seconds: spray paint, lunar surface, cattle, fire, videotape. The brand
was never recognised by its appearance; it was recognised by its **silhouette**. Absolute consistency
of form, absolute chaos of content.

**TumTum's turn on it:** MTV filled the M by hand, one artist at a time. TumTum fills it with data.
Every user generates their own fill — their heart-rate curve from that night, at that show. It is
the first container identity where the variation is produced by the audience, automatically, one per
event.

---

## 2. Audience

**Core: the night collector.** 18–34, São Paulo metro area. Attends six or more events a year.
Already owns a smartwatch and uses it mostly for notifications, not training. Posts a story at every
show and then does nothing with it. Keeps tickets, festival wristbands, setlist screenshots.
Does not want health. Wants a story.

| Tribe | Who | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| **Fandom** | Swifties, Coldplayers, K-pop, sertanejo | Highest viral coefficient. The only tribe actively waiting for artist comparison. Priority 1. |
| **Torcida** | Palmeiras, Corinthians, São Paulo, Flamengo | Highest recurrence — the calendar already exists. Shares in group chats more than in feeds. Priority 2. |
| **Festival-goer** | Lollapalooza, Rock in Rio, Primavera, raves | Collector by nature; 2–3 day arcs produce a series, not a card. Huge acquisition spikes on known dates. Priority 3. |

**Secondary (Phase 1):** artists, clubs and promoters. They do not buy the product — they become it.

**Explicitly not our audience: the quantified-self crowd.** Whoop, Oura, Garmin and running-Strava
users measure to improve; our audience measures to remember. Any drift toward performance, sleep or
recovery turns the brand into another health app, in a market where we have neither hardware nor
capital to compete.

---

## 3. Competitive position

There is no direct competitor. There is a perimeter: brands that measure the body and speak about
health, and brands that store memory and speak about emotion. The corner where both meet — live,
in-venue, involuntary — is empty.

| Reference | Does well | Leaves open |
|---|---|---|
| Strava | Turned body data into social proof. Closest behavioural precedent. | All effort and merit. No passive emotion, no culture, no night. |
| Spotify Wrapped | The gold standard for personal data as shareable content. | Yearly, aggregate, retrospective. Never about one specific moment you lived. |
| Whoop / Oura / Apple | Precision, trust, an established health language. | Cold, clinical, private. Nobody posts a Whoop screenshot because they were moved. |
| Setlist.fm / Songkick | The factual memory of the show. A partner, not a rival. | They keep what happened on stage. Nobody keeps what happened inside you. |
| Stories / BeReal | In the person's hand at the exact moment. Competes for the same attention. | Posed content that expires in 24h. A heartbeat neither poses nor expires. |

### Differentiation

1. **It is involuntary.** You pose for a photo; you do not pose a heartbeat. It is the only souvenir
   of an event that cannot be faked — and therefore the only one with evidentiary value.
2. **The data already exists.** Nothing to buy, nothing to remember to switch on. The watch was
   already on the wrist. We fetch it, cross it with the event timeline, and hand it back with meaning.
3. **It compares to the stage.** Nobody copies artist comparison without direct label and promoter
   relationships. That is the Phase 1 moat — and the reason the brand must look big before it is big.

### The feeling to trigger

One word: **arrepio** (chills). The instant of recognition — *"I knew that moment was different, and
now I have proof."* Every choice — colour, type, sound, copy, motion — is judged against one question:
does this move closer to that second, or further from it?

Temperature: nocturnal, sweaty, collective. Never clean, clinical or calm.

## 4. Personality

Archetype mix: **Creator 60%** (the variation engine) · **Jester 30%** (fun, bold, loud — what keeps
it from becoming a health app) · **Lover 10%** (a heartbeat is the most intimate thing someone can
hand over).

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Loud | Aggressive |
| Intimate | Invasive |
| Exaggerated | Dishonest |
| Precise with data | Clinical in manner |
| Brazilian without costume | Postcard-tropical |

The only dial pinned at zero is **clinical**. Everything else flexes by context: quieter on a consent
screen, louder on a share card.

## 5. Logo

Two parts, never separated in the primary version.

- **O Bloco** — the fixed geometry: `TUM` in a heavy slab cut, three letters packed into a single
  silhouette. The M's central chevron drops deep, reading as the valley between two beats.
  This is the container.
- **O Risco** — the second beat: `tum`, hand-drawn, always crossing the lower right at −5°, always
  with a knockout halo so it survives any fill behind it.

### Golden rule

> **Shape is law. Fill is free.**

Anyone may invent a new fill — a guest artist, an intern, an algorithm, a fan. Nobody ever touches the
geometry. That asymmetry is what let MTV survive four decades of changing taste.

### Fill categories

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Data** | The user's HR curve from that event. The default fill on every individual card. |
| **Photo** | Crowd, city, stage, pitch. Video too — the Bloco accepts a video mask. |
| **Texture** | Chrome, VHS scanline, halftone, noise, leopard. The 80s reference drawer. |
| **Event** | Confetti, striped turf, club colours, tour album palette. |
| **Flat** | A solid palette colour. The default at small sizes and for the favicon. |

### Versions

| Version | File | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | `logo/tumtum-primary.svg` | Signature, cards, anywhere with vertical room |
| Icon | `logo/tumtum-icon.svg` | App icon, favicon — the Bloco becomes a square, the Risco carries it |
| Horizontal | `logo/tumtum-horizontal.svg` | Nav bars and narrow strips; the Risco sits beside the Bloco |
| Seal | `logo/tumtum-seal.svg` | Stickers, wristbands, credentials, merch — the only use where the Risco stands alone |
| Block / Risco | `logo/tumtum-block.svg`, `logo/tumtum-risco.svg` | Raw parts for composition |

### Rules

**Always allowed:** swap the fill for any colour, texture, photo, video or pattern; bleed the Bloco
off the edge; animate the fill at the person's real BPM; redraw the Risco by hand for a campaign as
long as it still reads "tum"; knock the Bloco out of a crowd photo.

**Never:** change proportion, stroke or spacing of the Bloco; stretch, skew, round the corners, or add
shadow or bevel; rotate the Bloco (only the Risco is angled, and it is fixed at −5°); use the Risco
without its knockout over photo, video or busy texture; replace the Risco with an off-the-shelf script
font; write "Tumtum" in running caps as a substitute for the logo.

**Clear space:** 1×x on every side, where x = the Bloco's stem thickness (58 units). Suspended on one
side only for a deliberate bleed.

**Minimum size:** 28px block height on screen · 20px in a nav bar · 16px favicon (icon, flat fill) ·
14mm in print · 30mm embroidered (seal). Below 28px the Risco loses legibility — use the icon.

## 6. Colour

Three fixed core colours, five that rotate. **One carnival colour per surface — never two fighting on
the same screen.** Full values in `tokens.json` / `tokens.css`.

| Role | Name | Hex | Contrast on ink |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | Preto Palco | `#0A0A0F` | — |
| Brand | Batida | `#FF2E3C` | 5.4:1 |
| Text | Luz | `#F4F2F7` | 17.8:1 |
| Artist line | Palco | `#00E5FF` | 12.8:1 |
| Secondary text | Névoa | `#8A87A0` | 5.7:1 |
| Carnival | Ácido `#D4FF3D` · Choque `#FF3DBE` · Sol `#FFCC00` · Ultravioleta `#7A3DFF` · Campo `#00E676` | | 17.1 · 6.3 · 13.1 · 3.7 · 11.8 |

Ultravioleta fails AA for body copy at 3.7:1 — restrict it to large display type, icons and fills.

**The red rule.** Batida means two visceral things: *heart* and *stop*. In error states it always
carries an icon and text, never colour alone, and no other element on that screen may use red — so
the only red patch is the one that matters.

**Cyan is reserved.** When cyan appears, someone is on stage. It is never decoration.

## 7. Typography

| Role | Family | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Bricolage Grotesque** | Variable width and weight axes — the logo's principle applied to text. Headlines only, never paragraphs. |
| Text | **Instrument Sans** | All interface copy. Open enough to hold Portuguese accents at small sizes. |
| Data | **Martian Mono** | Every number that represents someone's body — BPM, time, sync percentage. Numbers are never Instrument Sans. |

All three are on Google Fonts under open licences and load through `next/font/google`.

Scale: display XL `clamp(2.5rem, 8vw, 6rem)` (wdth 100, tracking −3%, leading 0.92) · screen title
1.75–2.25rem (wdth 92) · card title 1.125rem (wdth 88) · body 1rem/1.6 at max 65 characters ·
label 0.68rem uppercase Martian, tracking +18% · large numbers free size, tabular-nums, tracking −6%.

## 8. Tone of voice

UI strings ship in Brazilian Portuguese. The test: if the sentence would fit in a WhatsApp group at
2am, it is TumTum. If it would fit in a health-plan leaflet, throw it away.

1. **Feeling, never health.** The word is *batida*, not heart rate. *Arrepio*, not physiological response.
2. **Short as a shout.** Headlines under twelve words; notifications under two sentences.
3. **Onomatopoeia welcome.** The name is already a sound. One per piece.
4. **Street Portuguese.** *A gente*, not *nós*. English only where it is already local vocabulary.
5. **The person is the subject.** "Seu coração bateu 187" — never "nós detectamos um pico de 187".
6. **Zero medical advice.** Hard rule, no exceptions. We never interpret, diagnose, reassure or alarm.

| We say | We don't say |
|---|---|
| batida, batimento | frequência cardíaca, BPM médio |
| pico | anomalia, evento detectado |
| show, jogo, festa | evento (only when unavoidable) |
| seu coração | seus dados biométricos |
| conectar o relógio | sincronizar dispositivo vestível |
| a gente | nós, a Tumtum, a plataforma |

## 9. Taglines under test

| Tagline | English | Bet | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **O show que seu coração viu.** | The show your heart saw. | Memory | Institutional, video, top of funnel |
| **Prova que você sentiu.** | Proof you felt it. | Social proof | Cards, performance, acquisition |
| **Bateu, ficou.** | It beat. It stays. | Sound | Merch, short signature, audio |

**Test plan (3 weeks):** three landing variants identical except the tagline, equal traffic split;
paid social with identical creative, audience and budget, minimum 3,000 impressions per variant;
decide on **completed signup with a connected watch**, not clicks — clicks measure curiosity,
connections measure conviction. Tie-break qualitatively: 48 hours later, ask 20 people which line they
remember. Recall beats a statistical tie.
